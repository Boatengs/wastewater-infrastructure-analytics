# Project Charter

## Project
**Wastewater Infrastructure Analytics**

## Consulting question
Which wastewater assets show the strongest evidence of condition, capacity, failure, and consequence risk; what maintenance, rehabilitation, or replacement pathways should be evaluated; what are the planning-level lifecycle cost implications; and how should limited capital funding be prioritized?

## Decision users
- wastewater utility managers and operators
- consulting engineers and planners
- asset-management and capital-improvement teams
- municipal finance and infrastructure-funding programs
- maintenance, collections, and treatment operations teams

## Workstreams

### Asset evidence base
Build an auditable asset inventory that preserves source identifiers, condition observations, inspection history, work orders, hydraulic/capacity indicators, and cost fields.

### Engineering-risk prioritization
Rank assets using transparent likelihood-of-failure and consequence-of-failure evidence rather than a black-box score.

### Rehabilitation and replacement strategy
Connect high-priority assets to feasible intervention pathways such as cleaning, inspection, spot repair, lining, rehabilitation, replacement, redundancy, or capacity improvement.

### Lifecycle economics
Translate planning assumptions into low/base/high capital and O&M scenarios while keeping unit-cost assumptions explicit.

### Capital planning
Prioritize candidate projects under constrained annual or multi-year funding scenarios and test sensitivity to risk, cost, readiness, and policy assumptions.

## Standards
1. Preserve raw utility data unchanged.
2. Separate measured observations, engineering assumptions, modeled quantities, and recommendations.
3. Keep every risk score decomposable to its component inputs.
4. Do not treat missing inspection or condition data as evidence of good condition.
5. Keep source-system identifiers as text unless a numeric type is technically required.
6. Present costs as planning-level estimates unless supported by current design documents or bids.
7. Document uncertainty, data gaps, and sensitivity.
8. Keep large, sensitive, or restricted utility datasets out of the public repository.
